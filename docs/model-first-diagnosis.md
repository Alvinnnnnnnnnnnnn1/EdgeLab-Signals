## What caused the 23-selection incident

The complete retained slate contains 814 quote-side observations and 250 canonical fixture/player/prop families. All 814 refer to the active V1.4.7 comparison journal, not `experimental_forecasts`. A previous original-store-only audit could not evaluate that population correctly. The new archive resolver verifies the active forecast, same-input control, immutable body hashes, feature manifests and pregame source clocks. It does not substitute an earlier original forecast with a different cutoff.

Replaying all original numeric/source/window gates produces 37 eligible observations across exactly 23 families, matching all 23 issued recommendations. Thirteen belong to Spurs–Kings. These are distinct player/prop families, rather than repeat recommendations for one family. The live loop emits every qualified family; it has no aggregate fixture or cross-prop player limit. Permanent family deduplication therefore behaved as specified while allowing concentrated exposure.

The observed result was 7 wins / 16 losses, with hypothetical −A$50.50 at A$5 each. Frozen probabilities imply 15.898706 expected wins conditional on participation. Selected predictions have Brier 0.36850. This serious selected-population discrepancy is not equivalent to a global model-calibration conclusion: observations share players, fixtures, assumptions and one date.

The Spurs–Kings forecasts were frozen approximately 12.6–12.8 hours before issuance. At the captured issue clock, the latest retained Fox/Wembanyama reports still said Questionable, not confirmed Out. No retrospective news is inserted into features. Starting-role fields are unavailable and remain unknown. Historical minutes and rates drive the frozen forecast; no validated quantitative response to teammate absences is established.

Castle's points mean was about 16.62 versus 28 actual. At the frozen implied rate, extra minutes explain about 3.94 of the 11.38-point error; the remaining 7.44 reflects rate variation and noise. Champagnie's mean was about 10.25 versus 22, with approximately 1.86 attributable descriptively to extra minutes and 9.90 remaining. These are postgame decompositions, not causal or counterfactual forecasts. Minutes alone do not explain the failures.

## Qualification and policy research

First rejection counts: probability range 416; minutes volatility 118; heuristic sensitivity 84; estimated EV 62; minutes mean 42; price range 25; current-player availability 24; disagreement 6. The complete CSV retains every observation and every simultaneous reason. These counts plus 37 eligible observations sum to 814. Structural identity, raw quoted-price/source, terms, source clocks and quiet-hour rules are replayed separately from numeric policy rules. Outcome evidence errors never enter pregame selection.

Chronological policy replay uses only the original captured quotes and frozen probabilities. Unchanged production selects 23 families (7/16, hypothetical ROI −43.91%). Removing only the heuristic sensitivity penalty selects 73 (35/38, −13.56%). The predeclared stricter history/minutes policy selects 17 (5/12, −45.53%). The latter does not establish that tighter gates improve quality; the former does not validate loosening. Both are inspected, correlated development evidence. No production policy or risk limit is selected from these returns.

Fixture/player cap sensitivities and explicit dependence extremes are retained separately. Their purpose is to measure concentration and demonstrate why independent Bernoulli inference is unjustified. They are not fitted dependence models, inferential p-values or independent validation. The optional transactional exposure governor remains disabled until an explicit justified owner policy is approved.

Decision: keep existing production weights and formal promotion gates. Ship validated diagnostic/source/authority repairs only after deployment safety passes. Prospective source/role evidence and independently graded dates are needed before promoting a statistical or policy challenger.
